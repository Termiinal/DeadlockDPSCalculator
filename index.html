How to use

This tool answers one question: which item should Wraith buy next? Describe your build, pick the items you're considering, and it simulates fights to show which one adds the most damage.

  1. Set your baseline stats. In Base values, enter your boons, headshot rate, and any weapon damage, fire rate, or spirit you already have.
  2. Toggle abilities. Turn Full Auto and Card Trick on or off, and tick any AP upgrades you have leveled. You can also click the card slots to start the fight holding cards.
  3. Choose what items/stats to compare. Each stat slider in Comparison items set above 0, and each item you switch on, adds its own row to the table. If you already own an item, tick its add to baseline box instead: every row then includes it, so the table shows what's best to buy next.
  4. Table explanation. Each row is your build plus that one item or stat, and the highlighted cell in each column is the best result. DPS @3s is a short burst, the next column uses your engagement length, and Total dmg is the damage dealt over that fight. With Time to kill on, the table shows seconds to kill instead, where lower is better. Click any row's name to see where its damage comes from. Below the chart, Stat weights show what one more point of each stat is worth to your baseline.
Modeling details
  • Weapon damage is reduced by bullet resist and spirit damage by spirit resist; shred subtracts from resist, which can go negative and amplify.
  • Bullet damage is (5.64 + 0.14·boons) × (1 + Weapon Damage %); headshots deal ×1.65 on bullet damage only. Boons also give 1.1 spirit each.
  • Numbers are averages over many seeded simulated fights, so the same inputs always give the same results.
  • Abilities and active items are used once per fight at the start (Full Auto first) (Sleep Dagger first, then Bullet Dance) (Threadsap first, then Baba's Brew), unless an item lets you set its timing. No recasts.
  • Sleep Dagger lands before the first bullet; it isn't a bullet, so no falloff or Plated Armor. Its 1AP shred lowers bullet resist only, for 6s (extended by duration bonuses).
  • Fixation stacks build by their average per bullet (1, or 3 on a headshot), and each bullet's bonus uses the stacks already on the target before it hits. The bonus can headshot and gets Lucky Shot and falloff; its per-stack value grows with Weapon Damage % (including Intensifying Magazine's ramp) but isn't multiplied by it. The 1AP bursts trigger at every 20 stacks up to and including max, then stop while you stay at max; they're spirit damage and fall off with range like other on-hit effects.
  • Bullet Dance's duration uses your spirit when it's cast. While it lasts, every bullet is a body shot and adds 1 Fixation stack; its flat damage gets Lucky Shot but not Weapon Damage %.
  • Threadsap lands before the first bullet. Its stolen spirit lasts the whole fight and counts for everything that scales with spirit, abilities and items alike: it steals 10 + 0.9·boons per target hit, capped at 45 + 2·boons, and you gain 175% of that (the target count only changes the steal). The 50 cast damage doesn't fall off; the on-hit damage falls off and Plated's 50% roll can block it. Debuff Remover returns all of the stolen spirit, from every target hit, and the on-hit stops.
  • Baba's Brew is cast at the start. Its damage ticks continuously for 8s (extended by duration bonuses) using your current spirit, and its spirit resist shred applies to all of your spirit damage, including the Brew itself. Debuff Remover ends it.
  • Cards build 3.4% per body hit and 5.1% per headshot, and are thrown as soon as they're ready (0.6s apart); a starting hand throws left to right. Suits come from a 2-of-each deck plus a 1/9 Joker chance (1/5 at 5AP): Spades ×1.6, Diamonds −7% both resists for 5s, and a Joker counts as every suit.
  • Fire-rate bonuses add together; separate damage reductions multiply.
  • Lucky Shot, Plated Armor, and Armor Piercing Rounds use their average effect rather than rolling each bullet. Lucky Shot doubles body-shot damage only; the headshot bonus and spirit on-hit effects aren't doubled. With its Ignore Plated option on, lucky bullets skip Plated Armor entirely: no deflect and no 50% block.
  • Headhunter's bonus goes on the first headshot after its cooldown (each bullet rolls your headshot rate; no headshots during Bullet Dance). It's weapon damage reduced by bullet resist and raised by shred, and Inhibitor still reduces it; Weapon Damage %, the headshot multiplier, Lucky Shot, falloff, Armor Piercing and Plated's deflect don't touch it. Plated's 50% roll can block it, leaving it ready for the next headshot.
  • Opening Rounds needs Time to kill, since it depends on the target's health: its +25% adds to Weapon Damage % while the target has more than half its HP left (HP actually lost, with Bloodletting), so it also raises Fixation's per-stack value.
  • Spirit Rend's 8% shred starts once your first bullet hits and refreshes with every bullet. Headshot stacks (+7% each, up to 4 for 36% total) share one 8s timer (extended by duration bonuses): each headshot off the 2s cooldown adds a stack and refreshes the timer, even at 4 stacks, and all stacks drop if it runs out. Headshots roll your headshot rate per bullet (none during Bullet Dance), and Plated's 50% roll can block the shred or a stack.
  • Mercurial Magnum's bullets deal 20% + 0.38%·Spirit of base bullet damage as spirit; Tesla's shock deals 33 + 0.19·Spirit (0.2s cooldown); Escalating Exposure stacks at most every 0.7s. Duration bonuses extend ability and item durations, including shred.
  • Past 18m, bullet damage and on-hit effects fall off linearly to 10% at 52m (21.6m and 62.4m with Sharpshooter).
  • Mercurial's card bonus is added after the suit multiplier, and its bullet damage uses base damage before Weapon Damage %.
  • Focus Lens's echo only counts if it lands before the fight ends. Time to kill caps at 60s; a * marks rows that sometimes don't kill in time.
  • Stat weights compare your baseline with the same build plus +10% Weapon Damage, +10% Fire Rate, +10 Spirit or +5% shred, divided down to one point. Both runs share their random rolls, and ± is the noise that's left. Weights describe your current build: they shift as it changes and can jump near thresholds like stack caps.
  • Each item's key numbers are listed next to its toggle.
